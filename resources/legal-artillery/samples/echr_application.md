## SECTION F — ALLEGED VIOLATIONS

### Article 6 section 1 — Right to a fair trial (equality of arms)

The applicant submits that the denial of access to the search file violates the principle of equality of arms, as the prosecution has full access to the search documentation while the applicant is denied any access.

The Court has consistently held that equality of arms requires that each party be given a reasonable opportunity to present its case under conditions that do not place it at a disadvantage vis-a-vis the opponent (Natunen v. Finland, no. 21022/04, 31 March 2009; Garcia Alva v. Germany, no. 23541/94, 13 February 2001).

### Article 8 — Right to respect for private life and home

The applicant submits that the search of his home constitutes an interference with his rights under Article 8. The inability to verify the lawfulness of the search — due to the denial of file access — means that the applicant cannot effectively challenge whether the interference was "in accordance with the law" and "necessary in a democratic society."

The Court held in Dragojevic v. Croatia (no. 68955/11, 15 January 2015) that searches must be based on proper legal grounds and must not exceed the scope of the investigation.

### Article 13 — Right to an effective remedy

The applicant had no effective remedy at his disposal. The informal denial without a written decision prevented the applicant from pursuing any appeal or judicial review. The domestic legal system failed to provide a mechanism for challenging the denial of file access.

## SECTION G — ADMISSIBILITY (Article 35)

The application is submitted within 4 months of the final domestic decision. The applicant has exhausted all available domestic remedies, or alternatively, submits that no effective domestic remedy exists, as the informal denial without a written decision precludes any appeal.
